HOW IT WORKS

Platelet-rich plasma

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy uses a sample of a person’s own blood. The sample is processed in a centrifuge to separate its components and prepare a platelet-concentrated plasma fraction. That preparation may then be injected into selected areas of the scalp. The clinic lists PRP among its hair-loss services, but whether it is suitable depends on the cause and pattern of hair loss and an individual assessment. The clinician should explain the evidence for the proposed use, how the preparation is made, the number and spacing of sessions, expected response and the possibility of limited benefit. Temporary soreness, bruising or swelling can occur after injections; the treating clinician should discuss individual risks, alternatives and aftercare before treatment.

The right approach depends on an individual assessment. Discuss suitability, benefits, limitations, risks, recovery or follow-up, and alternatives with the treating clinician before making a decision.
