HOW IT WORKS

Understanding Fractional CO₂ Laser

A fractional CO₂ laser creates controlled columns of heat in the skin while leaving surrounding areas untreated. It is used for selected resurfacing concerns, but suitability depends on the skin, the condition being treated and the person’s medical history. A clinician should examine the area, discuss expected benefits and limitations, and explain whether another approach may be safer or more appropriate. Treatment can involve discomfort, redness, swelling, a recovery period and careful aftercare. Risks include infection, prolonged redness, scarring and changes in skin colour; pigment changes may be more likely for some skin tones. Sun protection and adherence to the clinician’s instructions are important during healing. Ask about the device, operator training, preparation, pain control, downtime, follow-up and the chance that more than one session may be considered. Results vary, and no specific outcome can be promised. The clinician should also review relevant medical conditions and medicines, explain the proposed plan in plain language, and agree on follow-up.

This page provides general information, not a diagnosis or personal treatment recommendation. Suitability, risks and recovery should be discussed with a qualified clinician.
