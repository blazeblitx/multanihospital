HOW IT WORKS

Understanding Laser Hair Reduction

Laser hair reduction uses concentrated light that is absorbed by pigment in hair and converted to heat around the follicle. Because hair grows in cycles, treatment is usually planned across multiple sessions, with timing and response varying by body area, hair colour and skin type. It can reduce hair growth, but results are not guaranteed to be permanent and maintenance may be discussed. Before treatment, a trained clinician should assess skin and hair characteristics, recent tanning, medicines and any history of skin reactions. The provider should explain device settings, protective eyewear, preparation and aftercare. Temporary redness or swelling can occur; burns, blistering, pigment changes or scarring are less common but possible, particularly if treatment is unsuitable or settings are not appropriate. Ask who performs the procedure, what experience they have with your skin type, the expected number of visits, risks and alternatives. The clinician should also review relevant medical conditions and medicines, explain the proposed plan in plain language, and agree on follow-up.

This page provides general information, not a diagnosis or personal treatment recommendation. Suitability, risks and recovery should be discussed with a qualified clinician.
